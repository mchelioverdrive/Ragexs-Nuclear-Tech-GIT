## Electrical and saved-state rules

`IRatedGenerator` is the common production export boundary. `getRatedOutputWatts()` describes the generation ceiling; `getExportLimitWatts()` describes transfer; `getTechnicalBufferQuanta()` and `getIntentionalStorageQuanta()` distinguish storage hardware. One persisted `OutputBudget` debits **all** cable networks, adjacent direct transfers and charging slots in the owning world tick. Computer APIs expose state/configuration, not a second power allocator. Repeated/backward ticks cannot renew a spent budget. Storage capacity is never the default generator export rating.

Two electrical ticks cover either machine/network ordering without offering meaningful autonomy. This is source-based ordering justification, not a measured load-transient result. Explicit admin capacity overrides and pack-defined storage remain possible. Matter Discharger intentionally retains battery-backed storage; creative converters remain exempt. DFC uses its fictional incoming conversion rate, and Flare has a separate legacy-drain export ceiling with a zero generation rating.

Lower capacities are admission limits. Existing electrical and fluid counts are never clamped down. Revised native tanks ignore an old saved **maximum**, retain the saved **fill**, stop accepting more above the current maximum and allow normal draining. Steam selectors cannot relabel a filled inlet or select a grade beyond its construction rating. Incompatible saved inlet stock is offered to output piping; only already admitted fractional thermal parcels may finish under the old grade. Existing mass, combustion credit, exhaust ownership and fractional work are separate from electrical buffers and remain saved. Unknown Mini source item NBT is retained verbatim and produces no electricity; it is never replaced with fresh isotope fuel.

Full small/industrial exhaust closes admission rather than interpreting inventory fullness as measured pressure and exploding. Boiler safety remains upstream. Leviathan retains its closed-valve backpressure trip and lever/controller reset; there is no rotor-speed/inertia simulation, so electrical saturation is not called overspeed. Old trip code 2 remains latched until the existing reset interaction and is displayed as legacy load rejection.

## Isotope heat, decay and useful outputs

One native pellet contains three billets, adopted as 2 kg isotope material; cladding is excluded. `IsotopeLedger` supplies thermal rows separately from 5% thermoelectric conversion. Fresh whole-pellet thermal watts are Pu-238 1,140; weak mixed-U/Pu 380; Tm-170 27,000; Po-210 280,000; Am-241 229; Sr/Y-equilibrium 1,860; Co-60 35,000; Pm-147 666; Ra-226 56. These are adopted engineering coefficients, not a daughter-chain transport/deposition simulation. Pu uses [DOE's approximately 0.57 W/g reference](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/NEGTN0NEAC_PU-238_042108.pdf); the Tm coefficient follows the [DOE/Mound nuclear-battery report](https://www.osti.gov/bridge/servlets/purl/4807049-6bvOmJ/4807049.pdf). Specialty/exotic rows without an adopted assay retain an explicitly nominal 114 thermal W per native strength unit, anchored to the Pu pellet. Their shared accounting is implemented; independent nuclide/chain calibration remains a separate nuclear-material task.

`PELLET_DEPLETION`, depletion products, native loaded-tick lifetime and configured decay/scaling switches are preserved. This pass does not silently replace accelerated game lifetimes with real years. Fractional thermal/electrical conversion and last-paid tick persist. Unused heat is rejected; offline elapsed time produces no electricity. Mini recipes preserve the Pu/Tm identity in the crafted block's persistent NBT, and breaking/placing retains the same source/age. Unknown old Mini/Power units adopt fresh Pu/Po sources once; this does not recover an unknowable original recipe, convert old energy into source mass or refill a depleted known source.

Radiolysis uses a shared isotope/process energy budget. The existing 20-MJ peroxide process consumes the same electrical account that can charge items/export. Successful sterilization debits 20 J directly from this tick's isotope heat before thermoelectric conversion. External process electricity remains supported. Existing tanks, source slots and pending paid chemical batches remain owned. RTG furnace/heater compatibility levels are still nominal legacy levels and remain visible separately from calibrated generator heat.

## Fuel reserves and combined cycle

The Diesel's 20-L service reserve is about 19.1 minutes at 200 kW on fresh diesel/32% LHV. The 80-L industrial piston reserve is about 6.0 minutes with steel or 24.2 minutes with a 38%-efficient set at 750 kW on high-grade diesel. Turboshaft 80 L kerosene is about 11.7 minutes at 1 MW/25%, falling with afterburning. Gas-turbine 6,000 standard L methane is about 21 seconds at full 10-MW chemical input; pipeline infrastructure supplies sustained baseload. It suffices for the existing approximately 29-second lower-load start. These are fuel-assay calculations, not measured autonomous runtimes. Two liters lubricant provide approximately 33 minutes at 0.06 L/min; prepaid sub-liter oil credit is counted within that inventory, not a new source.

The current illustrative full-load cycle is **approximately 3.910 MW net, 39.10% LHV**, using one Industrial Steam Turbine and a powered condenser. Starting with 10 MW chemical input gives 3 MW gross/2.94 MW after existing gas-turbine auxiliaries, plus 5.5 MW owned exhaust. The 90%-transfer HRSG needs 3,601,434 J/kg of source heat to produce 450°C superhot steam from cold water with 6 kJ/kg paid feed pumping. Feed is about 1.527 kg/s; existing 0.5% boiler blowdown leaves about 1.520 kg/s for 85% ×770 kJ/kg turbine expansion and 10-kJ/kg condenser work. The extra electricity is produced by external bottoming equipment, not a hidden gas-turbine efficiency multiplier. Heat quality, output room, auxiliaries, startup and flow limits still apply.

## Solar area and environment

`RenderSolarPanel` renders `models/machines/solar_panel.obj` at unit world scale. The mesh spans 5 m along its long axis; its dominant upward tilted panel plane totals approximately 5.00 m². The retained 1-kW nameplate is a declared design normalization of 5 m² ×1,000 W/m² ×20%, not a change to nuclear/thermal units. The current vanilla-compatible celestial angle is zero at noon and one half at midnight, so output follows squared positive cosine. Sky occlusion and native celestial/orbital irradiance remain the environmental boundary. This is an abstract daylight yield curve, not a new orientation, weather or continuous photovoltaic model.

## Saturation and physical operation

Electrical acceptance does not control every prime mover. This census includes native, registered legacy/exotic and pack-defined generators, plus internal oil-machine recovery. Values are fresh defaults at 20 TPS; explicit machine/recipe overrides remain authoritative. Buffers below are **joules**, not watts. Ordinary buffers are transfer allowances without battery hardware. **Matter Discharger alone has intentional native electrical storage** (twelve battery blocks); custom storage needs a pack definition whose construction supplies it. Gears, visual rotors and the Leviathan flywheel do not establish an electrical battery.

A = continues and rejects surplus; B = governed input; C = protected shutdown; D = finite exhaust/rejection handling. Classes can overlap. These are implemented abstractions, not a simulation of rotor inertia, bearing losses or pressure dynamics. Declared ambient rejection cannot be recovered as another electrical/thermal credit. Steam machines require room for actual spent-steam mass; upstream boilers retain independent fuel/heat/pressure ownership. Electrical fullness is never an overspeed measurement.

## Electrical distribution and deferred accounting

`PowerNetMK2` uses finite endpoint snapshots, linear endpoint work and at most one offer per destination per pass. Rejection never drives a retry loop. Each priority sees actual remaining supply; proportional allocation uses a wide intermediate only when long multiplication would overflow. Aggregate advertised supply/demand is bounded at `Long.MAX_VALUE`, and each provider debit stays within its individual offer. Accepted deliveries are settled even on diagnostic cutoff. Direct transfers and diode forwarding share the work guard. A network cannot re-enter its active distribution scratch through a diode circuit. Direct diode forwarding debits remaining throughput and clears recursion state in `finally`.

`IEnergyHandlerMK2.prepareEnergyTransfer()` settles deferred process consumption against **old stock** before admission/extraction computes a new absolute quantity. All 28 native deferred electrical accounting owners implement it; assembler/chemical descendants inherit it. Settling only inside `setStoredEnergyQuanta(old + accepted)` would overwrite consumed work with a stale value. Getters and GUI/container queries read cached state, never traverse/rebuild electrical networks.

`ElectricalWorkGuard` shares one million visits /250 ms across nested electrical calls and accumulates work across root distributions in the same owning-thread world tick. Topology separately retains its existing 4,096-work repair budget; stale/duplicate split-queue removals now count too. Passes above 50 ms and abnormal paths warn at most once /10 seconds globally, including with detailed diagnostics disabled. Reports identify dimension/network, cable/endpoints, topology-change count, source/destination, offered/accepted/rejected quanta, remaining source budget, visits/attempts, repeated active paths and elapsed time. `/ntmpowerstats` retains the latest abnormal report. Cutoff defers distribution without spending rejected energy or changing ratings; it is an emergency threshold, not normal pacing. No guard can interrupt a third-party callback blocked inside its own code; capture its JVM stack.

The electric furnace has no battery/capacitor/flywheel in its beryllium/copper/tungsten-coil/furnace recipe. Its internal admission allowance is two heater ticks: **800 J at the default 8 kW**, scaling with upgrades (3.2 kJ at 32 kW). The slot battery discharges into it; the slot does not charge batteries. Client packets carry installed demand so gauge and tooltip use the same real capacity. Old saved 50-kJ stock remains owned and drains; further admission stops while over capacity, and the gauge stays bounded. Heavy Stirling export is at most 2 kJ/tick across all ports; default furnace heating spends 400 J/tick. Capacity does not create a generator burst allowance.


Deferred work is scheduled at the **last affordable tick**, so demand becomes dirty in time for the next network phase. A paid partial job at the depletion boundary is retained until a following settlement observes an actual unpowered tick; evaluation alone cannot erase paid progress. The same exhaustion-boundary assumption is corrected in shared assembler/chemical lanes, legacy chemistry, arc furnace/welder, centrifuge, microwave, vacuum circuit and the four deferred oil-machine schedulers. Immediate depletion resets are deferred in the reset-on-starvation furnace/shredder/welder, legacy compressor and applicable oil/circuit paths. Paid physical batches keep their existing pause/ownership rules, and the centrifuge's special first-tick/strict threshold and drive-processor threshold remain deliberate. This changes when work is settled, not electrical work per recipe.


Same-instance retained block-state swaps preserve persistent electrical/fluid endpoint membership. Actual removal/chunk unload still detaches; changing a furnace's lit block is not a cable disconnect. The shared machine generation ledger is public so vanilla `MapStorage` can invoke its saved-data constructor on reload; generation counts and existing data formats are unchanged.
