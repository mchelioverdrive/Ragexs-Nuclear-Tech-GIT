# Process reference

Current default implementation at 20 TPS. Enabled recipe JSON, IMC additions and server configuration can change the defaults. Production source and the loaded recipe maps decide actual behavior. Use the [documentation index](../index.md) for player guides and related references.

## Execution and accounting

`ProcessRecipe` validates and snapshots the bill. `ProcessBatch` reserves inputs and retains paid work, heat and products. `ProcessMachine` commits through the native inventory, tanks and electrical/qualified-heat sources on the owning server thread. Fractional species outputs accumulate in `MassRemainder`; they are neither rounded up into free output nor discarded. All deliverable products must fit. A blocked batch pauses with its state retained.

Chemical input + sensible input + electrical work + process heat = chemical products + sensible products + rejected heat. Chemical LHV bounds available energy; it is not a full formation-enthalpy database for salts, minerals or nonfuel ions. Nuclear mass defects and external irradiation terms are recorded separately from chemical accounting.

Recipe counts are species-specific: liquid litres, standard gas litres, water-equivalent steam, material items or specialist aliquots. See [fluid reference](fluid_reference.md). Shared Chemical Reactor lanes use the existing combined 108-kW envelope electrically, with 6,000 standard gas L/min; basic aqueous reagents and water use 60 L/min, while oil/fuel and specialty liquid service retain 12 L/min. Native machines can have different ratings. Displayed process ratings use maximum paid ticks, not storage size.

## Heat-source ownership

Execution heat, material temperature and informational process temperature have separate meanings. A hot input already owns its sensible energy. `referenceTemperatureC` describes the process; only `temperatureC` with a real thermal duty qualifies an external source. Electrical equipment retains the reference without an external-temperature execution gate.

The native heater contact is a 3x3 multiblock with upward output. Empty space below a powered vessel does not establish a thermal interface. The following table describes requirements and their intended source ownership.

### Exact heater placement

For the Coker, ordinary/industrial Boiler, Blast Furnace, Combination Furnace, Steel Furnace, Crucible, Stirling Engine and Sawmill: center the heater's upward top contact directly below the machine's bottom controller core at `(x, y-1, z)`. The full heater layer occupies `x-1..x+1`, `z-1..z+1`, below all required machine blocks; lateral machine fluid/power ports remain available at the machine's own layer. Firebox, Heating Oven and heat-exchanger heater cores sit one block below the receiver. The two-high Oil Burner core sits two blocks below it, with its top proxy directly contacting the receiver. Leave its lower layer clear too. The Electric Heater has the same thermal contact but also needs clearance for its projecting electrical lug. Rotate side-access faces to keep fuel, fluid and electrical connections accessible.

Heating Oven import uses that same aligned underside contact; its finite imported allowance displaces internal firing. A custom machine uses its actual `cm_heat` component as the receiver: center the heater immediately below that component, with the entire 3x3 top layer clear of the controller and required components, and enough additional clearance for the chosen heater's height/lug. Invalid/obstructed custom ports do not establish an external interface. Nominal custom heat recipes still require a deliberately configured usable heat port; this correction does not invent energy units for arbitrary third-party generator/nominal recipes.

### API, persistence and conservation

`IProcessHeatReceiver` is explicit opt-in. The receiver supplies its intended contact; `ProcessMachine.heaterBelow` checks only the directly contacting tile and upward source orientation. `ProcessMachine.step` no longer searches below arbitrary machines or forwards heat through Fraction Tower stacks. Heat-disabled proxies cannot supply heat. Existing native thermal machines retain their deliberate bottom-contact implementations; integrated geothermal processing continues to pass its own owned source explicitly.

The seven powered petroleum families normalize stock and JSON bills at their equipment registration boundary. Deuterium and Dairy use native electrical bills. Their old saved owned batches preserve input ownership, products, duration, progress and total paid work-plus-heat credit when the remaining utilities become electrical. Only supplied process duty is reassigned: sensible feed energy, fuel chemistry, rejection, catalysts, yields and progression are unchanged. NEI reads the resulting native utility bill and retains informational temperature metadata.

Refinery HOTOIL already contains 561 kJ/L from upstream heating; its additional 163 kJ/L vaporization duty remains payable and becomes electrical alongside 36 kJ/L auxiliaries. Coker/boiler heat, hot-blast credit, molten feeds, compressor work and internal furnace fuel retain their existing owners. Asphalt already pays 21 MJ electrically in the reactor; it receives no second conversion. No native multiblock structure needs alteration. Model/shape, controller/proxy placement, native ports, construction/GUI paths and original heat-source documentation support these decisions; the original manual explicitly identifies the Coker and the bottom-contact thermal equipment. Geometry checks establish structural clearance, not in-world placement or visual acceptance.

Possible later balancing remains separate: adopted refinery vaporization and upgrading duties, Coker throughput versus heater rating, Dairy evaporation, isotope-separation duties, and nominal solid-furnace thermochemistry. High source temperature is an execution qualification only where a physical receiving interface exists. No calorific or throughput rebalance is included here.

## Industrial batch policy

RTM prioritizes useful Minecraft production for bulk construction, agriculture and common prerequisites. Ordinary buildings use compressed gameplay parcels; oil, nuclear processing and specialty technology retain stronger physical/progression constraints. Commodity reaction batches use spare vessel, feed, product and utility capacity without changing their reaction ratios. `ProcessRecipe.rebatch` multiplies the complete immutable bill, including excess reagents, carrier recovery, explicit losses, work and heat. It preserves pressure, temperature references and output mode. Callers explicitly choose each lot and a duration at least as long as the former cycle; there is no global multiplier or automatic scaling of configured recipes. Already reserved batches finish their saved bill; product remainders and stored stock retain their original quantities. Enabled recipe JSON remains authoritative: server owners must update their overrides from the regenerated underscore-prefixed templates to adopt new default lots. Overrides containing the old nominal vanadium template (1086) must adopt its physical bill before loading.

Vanadium form consolidation returns one ingot per powder and recovers its erbium addition. The former three-ingot output created metal. New construction-output physical identities define lighter, compressed building parcels while retaining old construction identities for saved bills and remainders. Native block/item/fluid identifiers, saved counts and isotope coefficients remain unchanged. Exact mass remainders continue to cover fractional salts/polymers and realistic losses; rounding a preview upward does not award that output.

## Rotary drive

Defaults 1/2 ordinary counts/tick, 0.2/0.4 kg/s; saved fractional spent mass; at most 4 counts/tick in overrides. Steam expansion uses 40% of the ordinary-to-spent work (20/40 kW shaft); unused enthalpy is rejected. Fuel heat is separately paid at 1.8 MJ per output ingot-equivalent, at most 500 kWth and 85% fuel efficiency.

## Physical-model boundaries

Iron preparation is owned by `SteelmakingProcesses`, Chemical Reactor template IDs 1600–1603 and the existing `ProcessBatch`/`ProcessMachine` transactions. The dedicated Mixer cannot mix three distinct solids, so the multipurpose reactor performs wet mixing and electrically supplied induration/reduction within its established 108-kW process ceiling and finite cooler. Iron powder conditioning explicitly admits oxide oxygen; hydrogen reduction debits reducing gas, returns only unused rounding surplus, and captures reaction water. Limestone decarbonation and the 100-g clay binder remain in the material bill. No fluid identities or existing solid coefficients change.

The Large Arc Furnace's solid DRI charge uses `ArcSteelBatch`: an immutable reserved count bill, separate from physical mass-based preparation because final steel recovery is deliberately gameplay-scaled. Eight native DRI, one coal coke and one quicklime atomically reserve against space for 32 steel and 2 slag. The batch owns exact tagged input stacks, outputs, required work and paid work in version-1 `realSimArcSteel` NBT. MachineRuntime advances paid work on the owning server thread, capped by the existing upgraded arc drive and actual buffer stock, including partial final debits. No RF or secondary power ledger exists. Missing power/electrodes, output blockage, recipe reload and unload retain the owned charge. Controller teardown releases either unfinished inputs or paid products exactly once. The existing legacy melting and molten-metal paths retain their recipes and saved fields; owned steel charges refuse mode changes. NEI's solid arc handler exposes all three consumables and both outputs.

This is oxide conditioning rather than an ore-beneficiation simulator: no gangue is invented when starting from clean metallic powder. Each new intermediate preserves one powder-equivalent count and has no general iron ore-dictionary alias or reverse route. Final pellet/DRI furnace yields must not be interpreted as the physical ledger's mass conversion factors. The route follows hydrogen reduction and subsequent electric melting as described by [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-04/Hydrogen%20Market%20Module%20-%20Component%20Design%20Report_0.pdf); temperatures, rates and gameplay recovery factors are explicit RTM parameters.

Crystallizer crystal/concentrate assays, metal-electrolyser mineral/gangue chemistry, geological solid sources, reactor burnup and exotic energy remain explicit game models. Solvent/acid baths return carrier with integrated regeneration; a bath charge is not bulk per-item consumption. Repeating recipe identities cannot turn depleted isotope reject or spent lithium targets into fresh natural feed. Crystallizer efficiency saves work, never grants free retained solid feed. Cooling is finite: the Chemical Reactor has 1 MW rejection and the industrial reactor shares 5 MW across lanes.

For consumer nameplates and closed cooling, see [manufacturing reference](manufacturing_reference.md). Configuration and saved-state rules are in [compatibility](compatibility.md).
