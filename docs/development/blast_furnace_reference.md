# Blast Furnace reference


The seven-high Blast Furnace is a coke-fired shaft furnace with downstream refining abstracted, separate from the coal-hungry **Metallurgical Furnace** (`machine_difurnace_*`, original tile IDs, inventories and saved fields preserved). Its shape, ports, OBJ, body texture and GUI art originate in HbmMods/Hbm-s-Nuclear-Tech-GIT at `29a29447b7d6c952fb05f97e616145081230074a`. The upstream generic recipe framework, arbitrary fuel counters, tank-fullness speed rule and exponential hot-air drain are not used. RTM's native recipe loader/NEI, MachineRuntime and shared prepared-model/VBO cache own these paths.

Construction is ordinary crafting: `BFB / BMB / PBP`, where B is a steel block, F a firebrick block, M a basic motor and P a steel pipe item: five steel blocks (45 ingot equivalents), one firebrick block, one motor, two pipes. Existing firebrick and motor construction remain available before petroleum/nuclear machinery.

The default industrial bills are:

| Feed plus one reserved reductant item | Steel | Slag | Process heat | Blast amount |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One iron-ore parcel + coal coke | 3 | 2 | 18 MJ | 2700 standard L |
| One iron-ore parcel + raw coal | 2 | 3 | 16 MJ | 2400 standard L |
| Two iron ingots/dust + coal coke | 2 | 0 | 8 MJ | 1800 standard L |
| Two iron ingots/dust + raw coal | 2 | 0 | 12 MJ | 2400 standard L |

Steel uses the existing 1-kg/ingot planning convention. The ore parcel preserves the inherited three-ingot recoverable-iron yield; raw coal loses one ingot-equivalent into slag. Ore and slag items remain nominal mineral parcels, not a newly asserted universal block mass. The old high-yield ore/any-coke **default** has moved; old lower-yield steel and all general-purpose alloys remain in the Metallurgical Furnace. Its fuel economy, extension, RTG variant, command/IMC identity and `hbmBlastFurnace.json` customization remain intact. Enabled user overrides are authoritative and can deliberately reintroduce the old route. Industrial customization has its own `hbmIndustrialBlastFurnace.json`, with explicit heat (J) and air (standard L) bills. Upstream red-copper, meteorite/starmetal, protective-plate and firebrick recipes are intentionally not imported into this ferrous machine.

Coke is both carbon reductant and heat source. Each 1-kg coal-coke item supplies 28.2 MJ LHV, partitioned into 55% owned process heat, 0.25% recoverable top-gas chemical energy and 44.75% rejection/unrecovered exhaust. Raw coal is 25.8 MJ/kg, partitioned 30%/0.125%/69.875%, with higher soot per consumed item. There is no auxiliary item-fuel intake. A minimum one-item recipe reductant charge remains intentional, with its entire chemical-energy partition recorded. Its heat credit persists across charges. Hot blast supplies sensible heat; any remaining deficit can be paid by a calibrated `IProcessHeatSource` immediately below the controller, at 900 C or hotter. This port represents burden preheating in the lumped duty model, not a claim that 900-C external heat alone melts iron or replaces carbon reduction. The source's temperature, stored heat and shared per-tick export budget all apply. A cold ore/coke charge needs about 2.49 MJ beyond its reserved coke; an un-enriched 800-C ore/coke charge closes its heat bill with that coke plus blast. Missing heat slows or pauses work without consuming unpaid air. Old slot-0 stacks remain in a retrieve-only return position, including automation extraction; saved thermal credit is retained. All original inventory indices remain stable.

Forced-air storage uses existing pressure class 1. The native compressor adds paid air-compression routes without changing existing gas/liquid identities. Boilers heat compressed air to 600 C, then 800 C, through qualified `IProcessHeatSource` draws. Outputs retain pressure. Heating requires a source at least 30 C hotter than the outlet. The existing 900-C combustion sources can supply these states. Air mass uses the existing dry-air assay, 1.281255 kg/m3 at 0 C and 1 bar; cp is approximated as 1 kJ/(kg K). Boilers conservatively round the heat debit up, while the furnace rounds delivered sensible heat down. No heating step creates mass or pressure, and full or mismatched output tanks retain their contents. Existing class-0 air can instead be heated and subsequently compressed.

Coke thermal ceilings are 2/4/8 MW for cold/600-C/800-C blast; raw-coal ceilings are 1.5/2/2 MW. The higher ceiling represents improved tuyere combustion and thermal conditions, while supplied blast enthalpy independently reduces fuel duty. Tank fullness supplies neither speed nor free heat. A full cold ore/coke batch takes nine seconds at its declared ceiling; hot blast takes 2.25 seconds when adequately supplied. Actual rates depend on reductant, available heat, pressure, blast flow, outputs and scheduling. An optional O2 supply initially sufficient for 10% of the normal air amount selects an enriched operating regime, giving 25% more thermal throughput and 10% less loss duty. This corresponds to roughly 28% O2 in the enriched inlet using the existing 21% air O2 assay. O2 supplies no chemical energy, is consumed with processing and is never a construction requirement. Enrichment pauses if its supply is interrupted.

Optional powdered limestone is adopted as a 1-kg CaCO3 charge for ore bills. `CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2` costs 1.8 MJ calcination heat; basic slag formation is assigned 3 MJ less integrated slagging/heat loss, for a net 1.2 MJ reduction in the lumped bill. This loss allowance is a declared gameplay design parameter, not a measured universal limestone saving. One extra nominal slag parcel represents the lime-bearing basic slag. CO2 mass follows 44.0095/100.0869 of the charge, about 226.913 standard L; fractional mass persists in the existing MassRemainder account and is released only with a completed paid batch. CO2 has a separate output tank and cannot become combustible top-gas energy.

Blast Furnace Gas uses a declared dry molar assay of 22% CO, 22% CO2, 54% N2 and 2% H2. Its LHV derives from the registry's CO/H2 molecular chemistry, about 2.954 kJ/standard L, rather than a generic FLUE heat trait. The nitrogen/CO2 dilution, CO toxicity and low calorific value are deliberate. Gas production follows removed fuel and its chemical partition, independent of elapsed processing time; fractional gas energy remains owned across save/load. It can supply the existing physical fluid burner and thus oven/boiler/hot-blast heat, or be flared. An open chimney vents at 200 standard L/s per output stream with pollution; covering the chimney requires recovery and pauses new fuel charging when storage is full. Gas cleanup, full elemental burden/offgas closure, water vapor, refractory wear and sensible top-gas heat recovery remain abstract. The dedicated gas is useful chemical recovery; its chemical identity does not imply a fully resolved elemental simulator.

The intended chemistry is oxide reduction (`Fe2O3 + 3CO -> 2Fe + 3CO2`), coke gasification (`CO2 + C -> 2CO`), limestone calcination and basic silicate slag formation (`CaO + SiO2 -> CaSiO3`). Real blast furnaces produce carbon-rich hot metal, not finished steel. Decarburization/refining and casting remain integrated here until RTM gains standalone Bessemer/open-hearth/BOF and molten-metal handling. Neither the Metallurgical Furnace nor Large Arc Furnace is required downstream. The [IEA steel roadmap](https://www.iea.org/reports/iron-and-steel-technology-roadmap) describes coke's reducing role and the separate BF/BOF route; [EPA AP-42](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-04/ap42_3rdsup14_may1983.pdf) documents combustible low-LHV blast-furnace gas. The fixed assay and machine envelopes above are RTM's adopted proxies.

The server reserves feed and reductant, snapshots the output/heat/air bill, advances only paid work and retains blocked charges. Versioned NBT owns process work, thermal credit, air/O2 debits, products and fractional gas/CO2. Removing a controller drops either its unfinished feed or fully paid products once; burned reductant and spent heat are not refunded. Client packets are display snapshots, not authoritative saved credit. World/chunk lifecycle uses the existing runtime owner. Inventory/fluid dirty events wake processing; scheduled one-tick accounting (so shared heater export limits are available on every active tick) advances active work without catch-up; twenty-tick connection/recipe-revision reconciliation retains existing network lifecycle conventions. Idle and blocked furnaces have no process transition. No second scheduler, per-tile server operation tick, legacy OBJ renderer or duplicate model upload owner is introduced.

Gas recovery is intentionally a small secondary benefit of pre-oil coke-fired ironmaking. The furnace is not a modern foundry; downstream refining remains abstract. Its large thermal ceilings describe rapid bulk processing of coal/coke chemical heat, not an electrical demand or a late-game technology requirement. Steel throughput, yields and paid fuel economics are independent of the recoverable-gas allowance.

One coke/three-steel hot-blast batch provides only **70.5 kJ** recoverable chemical gas energy, about **23.9 standard L**. At the un-enriched, un-fluxed **80-steel/min** bulk ceiling this is **31.33 kW chemical input**, or **26.63 kW useful heat** through the existing 85%-efficient burner. Alternatively, Stirling conversion gives approximately **8.99 kJ / 2.50 Wh** (normal 15%) or **11.99 kJ / 3.33 Wh** (heavy 20%) electrical energy per batch, about **4.0/5.3 kWe** at that throughput. Installed ratings and heat quality still apply. Oxygen plus limestone can raise furnace throughput to around 120 steel/min, giving about **6/8 kWe** recovered electricity; the byproduct remains below a normal 10-kWe Stirling nameplate. Gas burned for heating is unavailable for electricity. This deliberately limited capture allowance represents only the useful recoverable part of the early furnace's exhaust; most chemical/thermal energy is used in the process or rejected/unrecoverable. The furnace and flare generate no electricity, and the gas has no reciprocating-engine/gas-turbine fuel approval.
There is no default Blast Furnace Gas separation recipe. Its CO/H2 composition does not grant gas-turbine or reciprocating-engine approval.

## Air supply and operating information

The tier-2 anvil Atmospheric Compressor collects Earth's natural air at pressure class 0 (up to 150000 standard L/min, 125 kWe; 50 J/standard L). Its construction uses steel shells/plates, basic motors and pistons, steel pipe, copper wiring, Analog control and scaffold; it needs no titanium rotor, lubricant, petroleum or blast-furnace-only material. First steel remains obtainable through the Metallurgical Furnace. The existing Analog circuit route uses crafted tubes, capacitors, boards and solder; its insulating plates can be made from string and wool or fired bricks, so it does not require petroleum polymers. Pipe collected air into the separate Compressor, whose registered class-0 to class-1 air process has a 750-kWe drive ceiling. Its unchanged 328.94-kJ/1000-standard-L work bill completes in nine ticks at full supply (133333 standard L/min). Lower electrical supply pays partial work rather than imposing a 750-kW minimum; brownouts and blocked outputs retain paid work. Other physical conversions retain their 25-kWe ceiling and steam retains its continuous thermal account. Version-1 compressor progress migrates at the old 2500-quanta/tick payment, never the new drive rate. Bulk air collection also scales its parcel to supplied work, preserving its 50-J/standard-L cost; non-air bulk gases and selective collection retain their rates and specific work. NEI now exposes this route alongside registered phase/steam conversions and decompression. Supply enough storage and collection/compression capacity for the intended furnace duty.

Class-1 air can feed the furnace cold. Boilers preserve that pressure while heating it to 600 C, then 800 C using external paid process heat at 630 C and 830 C or hotter respectively; the ordinary firebox reaches 900 C. NEI includes both ambient-pressure heating and class-1 blast heating. Optional oxygen gas comes from air separation or water electrolysis at class 0; liquid oxygen must first be converted back into gas. The furnace's oxygen tank and cold/600-C blast tanks have stock gauges and quantity hovers; the 800-C blast and top-gas gauges retain their positions.

The compact LSHIFT item description lists every speed mechanism: coke rather than raw coal; hotter blast; coke-only oxygen enrichment (+25% thermal ceiling, -10% batch heat); and limestone with slag (-1.2 MJ batch heat). A full batch's oxygen must be available when the charge starts, at one standard L oxygen per ten standard L blast. Neither tank fullness nor additional stored energy is a speed multiplier. Output blockage or missing reductant/heat/blast pauses operation. The actual furnace progress silhouette exposes the paid heat, blast and oxygen counters on hover. These information changes preserve the existing process limits and paid material/energy accounting.

Cold, 600-C and 800-C air are alternative **pressure-class-1** blast supplies; heating is optional. The first admitted air grade blocks admission of the other two until its tank empties, while class-0 oxygen remains independently available for enrichment. Stored legacy mixtures are retained and consumed one grade at a time; refilling cannot add another mixture. Gas admission and heating are metered together, including small final parcels, so changing inlet grades does not require discarding a stranded partial charge. The last paid blast grade is retained in the batch NBT for integer-litre rounding credit; old saves use the conservative cold grade when that field is absent.

The default ore/coke charge uses 2.7 standard m3 per 3 kg output, or **900 standard m3/t**. Its ceilings therefore need 18000 L/min cold, 36000 at 600 C, 72000 at 800 C, or up to 108000 with oxygen and flux. These are reference-state volumes, not compressed pipe volume. They are plausible per mass of output: the base case in [ISIJ International 52 (2012), Table 2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/isijinternational/52/11/52_1979/_pdf) gives 8825 Nm3/min and 12136 t/day, about 1047 Nm3/t. Reference pressures differ slightly, and the modern plant is not a direct calibration of this small furnace. Minecraft's accelerated throughput, lumped heat chemistry and local class-1 10-bar proxy remain declared abstractions. No batch air bills or steel yields changed in this supply-capacity correction.

The flare rejects combustion heat and creates no electricity. Its retained electrical interface only drains pre-existing legacy stock; the 100-kW drain ceiling is not presented as a generation capability. A placed flare with such stock identifies it explicitly. Useful recovery requires the existing fluid burner/boiler route. This follows the [EPA flare fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-08/documents/fflare.pdf), which identifies combustion heat as lost in ordinary flaring.

Both Boiler variants share blast heating, oil/coolant heating and compatible legacy non-steam processes. Their fluid-identifier feed selector uses the same native eligibility checks, including air without a legacy heatable trait, and refuses to erase a nonempty inlet. The Industrial Boiler retains its higher transfer/steam-pressure capability and can also produce ordinary steam: with the output drained and no owned steam parcel, use a STEAM identifier for ordinary steam or HOTSTEAM for the industrial default. Outlet preference is saved; existing saves retain their previous default and pending parcel. The small Boiler remains limited to ordinary steam. The industrial 900-C burner can heat blast air, while external source temperature remains authoritative where used. NEI cycles both boilers for shared processes and restricts hot-steam pages to industrial hardware; unsupported steam-to-steam relabeling is no longer advertised as boiler heating. Steam previews include the existing 0.5% blowdown equivalent.
