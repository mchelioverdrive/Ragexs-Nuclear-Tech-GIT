# Nuclear weapon balance

RNT retains the existing cube-root conversion as its compatibility baseline: the 15 kt Little Boy-class yield maps to legacy radius 48. A legacy radius is a gameplay-scale input, not a universal blast radius and, at the MCHeli boundary, is **not** a literal kiloton value.

## Modular compatibility boundary

RNT now resolves a small immutable `NuclearBurstContext` from the world, detonation coordinates, and legacy radius. `NuclearBurstResolver` is deterministic, inexpensive, and internal to RNT; there is no pending-detonation registry and nothing new is reflected into MCHeli.

The legacy factories retain their signatures and independently resolve the same context:

* `EntityNukeExplosionMK5.statFac(World, int, double, double, double)` owns physical blast, thermal, prompt-radiation, terrain, and fallout work only.
* `EntityNukeTorex.statFac(World, double, double, double, float)` owns visual cloud/fireball state and delayed pressure-wave sound only.

Neither factory creates the other entity or requires the other call to have happened. Consequently, MCHeli's existing `NukeEffectOnly` path continues to produce a fully resolved Torex without MK5, while callers that suppress visuals can use MK5 alone. Existing MCHeli `nukeYield` values remain legacy radii and require neither migration nor a new weapon setting.

`ExplosionAltitude` needs no special bridge: it changes the coordinates supplied by MCHeli, and each RNT factory independently resolves the resulting burst height. No MCHeli `BurstType`, ground-coupling, fireball, fallout, or visual-profile variable exists or is required.

## Burst environments and effects

### Burial, containment, and venting

Subsurface resolution distinguishes resistance-based predicted breakthrough from actual surface breach, atmospheric release, ground coupling, and surface deformation. Prediction sizes mechanical excavation only; it cannot authorize flash, cloud, thermal ignition, pressure, fallout, or radioactive crater biomes.

Actual release requires a bounded (16,384-node, radius-capped) traversal through air or replaceable space to a sky-exposed block. It runs initially to recognize an existing shaft and authoritatively after all incremental ray chunks finish. Solid surviving roof blocks cannot be crossed. Confirmed outlet coordinates are persisted and used as the subsurface atmospheric and fallout source.

The named subsurface cavity coefficient is 0.60. Batched rays retain their existing strength, resistance-budget, generalized-spiral, legacy maximum-reach, incremental cursor, and per-chunk semantics; no artificial vertical shaft is cut.

Contained shots retain cavity excavation, ground shock, and distance-scaled seismic shake, but produce no Torex flare, cloud, sound, surface thermal or pressure effects, fallout rain, or radioactive crater-biome conversion. Confirmed release is scaled by its atmospheric factor; fallout requires a minimum 0.10 release. Surface and air bursts do not acquire a subsurface-breach requirement.

MK5 sends a saved one-shot seismic cue carrying hypocenter, yield, coupling, burial depth, duration, and intensity. Clients attenuate it by distance, respect the HUD shake option, use slower and longer motion, and select the stronger active shake rather than stacking. Torex remains independent and visual-only-capable.


## Static resolver scenarios

The following are resolver calculations, not runtime measurements. “Clear” means a height strictly greater than the listed fireball radius. The profile's soil-coupled crater radius is the current full crater coefficient (`legacy radius * 0.42`) multiplied by coupling. A fully clear airburst has a zero soil-coupled crater coefficient and retains the 0.080 atmospheric fallout multiplier; this does not veto blast damage to reachable terrain or buildings. Surface fallout uses coupling (subject to fission/salted legacy settings).

| Little Boy above clearance | 48 / 15.00 kt | 17 / 16.80 blocks | AIR / 0.000 | 0 / 0.080 | Altitude-origin atmospheric cloud |

The MCHeli example intentionally demonstrates compatibility conversion: radius 90 converts to about 98.88 kt with the existing RNT curve, even if a weapon is described elsewhere as “150 kt.” RNT does not reinterpret that existing configuration value.

## Incremental terrain processing

MK5 initializes the single batched ray engine at the floored detonation coordinates, independently of Torex visibility or animation. Surface and subsurface strength retain the coupled crater and cavity coefficients respectively. Airbursts use the existing full surface strength scale (`legacy radius * 0.42 * 2`), without changing the airburst soil-entrainment or fallout coefficients. Rays must actually reach a solid block and retain positive resistance energy; high bursts do not project destruction onto the surface.

Workers process disjoint contiguous reservations of up to 8,192 rays, with at most min(4, configured worker count) outstanding batches per explosion. The unchanged 32,768-iteration calculation cap bounds each batch; the larger ray cap allows cheap rays to use that allowance instead of returning after 256 rays and waiting for another server tick. Cancellation is checked every 32 iterations. MK5 no longer adds the legacy inverse-strength completed-ray allowance on top of its server time budget. The server reserves the saved direction sequence incrementally, merges masks and adopts range checkpoints. Worker and fallback paths use the same versioned mapping. All world reads, callbacks, snapshot preparation/validation and mask application remain on the server thread. The global 64-batch limit (16 slots reserved for resumptions), server-wide 5 ms default budget, chunk-loading allowance and snapshot-memory ceiling remain authoritative. Unavailable chunks retain their cursors; workers are never joined or polled in a wait loop.

Each range has separate bounded calculation scratch. Voxel-prefix checkpoints through at most 256 samples retain exact float energy, last positive sample index and qualified chunks. Conservative direction intervals prove identical ordered voxels, or an interval wholly inside a proven zero-resistance vanilla-air section; every referenced immutable section version must be present before reuse. Ambiguous float boundaries use normal traversal. A fixed 4,096-entry cache reuses exact `Math.pow` results keyed by sampled resistance and integer distance. Neither mechanism uses destruction masks to omit resistance. Reconstruction receives private copies of committed destruction coverage and can jump over a section only when all of its non-air, non-liquid blocks are already represented; tip priority is still merged separately. These reduce repeated calculation without changing spiral density, radius, resistance rules or endpoint reconstruction geometry. The worst case for arbitrary heterogeneous terrain remains cubic in strength; no subcubic runtime bound is implied.

Batches receive up to 128 immutable section inputs from a range's own working set. They can retain up to 128 additional already-captured sections through a concurrent directory keyed only by primitive world-lifecycle, calculation-view and section coordinates. That lookup performs no world reads or captures. Retaining and retiring synchronize on the same buffer; retirement removes the published version, and reader-held buffers stay charged until release. This avoids server-tick round trips and cyclic corridor eviction for long paths even when their data is already cached. Dynamic inputs receive the same server-side validation as initial inputs. A missing section can prepare up to four continuation sections in already-loaded terrain under the same deadline; this does not load speculative chunks.

Null vanilla-air sections use compact uniform snapshots with no block arrays or per-block capture callbacks. Capturing a chunk also retains its known null-section mask, avoiding repeated chunk loads solely to discover air at another altitude. Block/render changes clear affected metadata, chunk loads refresh it and world unload discards it. Exact palettes store up to 256 distinct resistance/flag pairs in populated sections; sections with more values expand to full float/flag arrays under the same byte ceiling. Accounting includes null-section metadata, capture scratch, expanded arrays and retired reader-held buffers. Section and column metadata maps each have a 16,384-entry hard cap, independently of the configured memory budget. Completed snapshots do not retain chunk objects and remain reusable after chunk unload; subsequent chunk loading requires validation. Block/render notifications mark affected samples for budgeted validation. Unchanged resistance and air/liquid classification preserve the immutable data and calculated results; actual changes reject the affected batch immediately, without loading its remaining inputs. Three rejections without committed progress finish only the current ray through bounded server fallback, then return the range to workers. A worker exception keeps that failed range in fallback instead; failures are counted separately. NBT retains unfinished reservations, range cursors, fallback boundaries, per-ray qualifications and committed masks. Calculation scratch, snapshot directories, null-section facts and prefetch hints are disposable; uncommitted work is recalculated on reload against the saved retained originals.

Thermal ignition continues independently. Collection/adoption and excavation alternate first turn and split each existing scheduler slice; either stage can run while the other waits. The first validated masks can remove blocks before unrelated ray ranges finish. Destruction coverage, pending removal, completed removal and terminal notification state are separate, so later results can add work to processed sections or upgrade a completed normal hit to one terminal effect. Rejected removals remain pending. Fallout follows all calculations, mask adoption, removals, terminal effects and required breach confirmation; thermal ground ignition retains its separate completion requirement.

Each calculation view journals exact original float resistance, vanilla-air/custom-air/liquid flags and source block ID immediately before its own progressive removals. Sorted primitive section arrays survive ordinary snapshot eviction and are saved with the masks and checkpoints. Both workers and bounded server fallback read the same view: only journaled cells still containing the expected vanilla air receive original values; unrelated replacements and other changed cells retain normal snapshot validation. Nothing treats destruction coverage as permission to skip attenuation. Retained originals share the configured snapshot byte ceiling and may consume at most half of it for new removals, leaving calculation space. When reservation fails, only affected excavation waits; rays and other jobs continue. Reload restores all required originals before either stage advances and waits rather than dropping dependencies if the ceiling has been reduced. Completed crater masks retire after ray collection and their owned removals/effects finish. Original resistance has separate ownership: pressure retains excavated terrain throughout its possible shielding footprint until selection ends; pending callbacks retain source IDs. Unowned originals are then reclaimed under the deadline. Cancellation/world unload releases all transient state.

Progressive eligibility follows removal callbacks, separately from mining-drop overrides. Audited RNT ores, ordinary outgas blocks (excluding ancient scrap), waste/scorched terrain and ordinary RNT plants progress immediately. Native stairs are eligible only when their delegated model removal is audited. Saved deferred cells are re-audited once under the application budget. Native classes must inherit base removal callbacks; native logs/leaves use their normal bounded metadata-only decay callbacks and never use the column bypass. Tile entities and unknown removal callbacks wait for both selectors. Fragile attachments are journaled and removed before their supports, with completed mask bits and saved notifications.

New detonations permute the existing equal-area Fibonacci sphere with a recursively interleaved, deterministically scrambled index order. Early bounded prefixes span elevation and azimuth; lookup needs O(log(ray count)) work and no direction array. Each index executes once, including counts not divisible by three. Direction-order version 2 is saved explicitly. Existing version-1 latitude-third sweeps and version-0 generalized-spiral recurrences resume their saved cursors and angles unchanged. Ray count, strength, attenuation, reach and final direction set remain unchanged from version 1. Section application starts at burst height and alternates above/below it.

The atmospheric front feeds exposed-block selection into the same crater masks and scheduler. It rejects whole chunks outside the circular footprint before loading, bounds discovery by top-filled storage and retained originals, skips proven empty sections, and streams one charged section candidate batch at a time. Top-down column barriers stop discovery below geological terrain or liquids, including excavated original terrain. Block/render changes and chunk reloads dirty pending candidate data; live targets are checked again before committing. Existing pressureTarget material rules, reach, resistance, raw protected-block checks and per-voxel shielding costs remain authoritative. Original-view zero-air intervals skip individual shielding samples. Bounded exact prefix checkpoints reuse identical voxel paths only with equal initial pressure and validated section versions; ambiguous paths use full sampling. Pressure discovery and traversal use the shared deadline, with no fixed per-tick operation cap. An old saved column/Y cursor migrates by excluding its already-visited cells; unfinished candidates restart from restored originals.

Nuclear removal skips mining drops, removes fragile attachments before support, clears destroyed inventories before break callbacks, and uses exception-safe server-thread callback scopes. Forge harvest events suppress scoped drops; spawn events catch direct scattering while excluding player/death paths. A per-dimension saved journal holds at most 65,536 exact affected leaves, unsupported plants and natural falling blocks for delayed updates, with bounded pruning and no terrain loads or item-area searches. Capacity exhaustion delays the attributable mutation. Player harvesting, death inventories, ordinary item spawning and saved items loaded by a nested callback remain unaffected. Fallout transformations and its existing capped collapse entities use the same suppression. No additional debris is introduced.

Column batches retain counted section writes, the native highest-cell height/gap update, saving and every required watcher notification. Native zero-emission/zero-opacity air skips a light queue call only when the saved value already equals Forge's computed value; changed lighting and fallback cases retain native updates. These are architectural changes, not measured performance guarantees.

The final generalized-spiral pole uses a finite zero longitude. Existing saves paused on its former NaN direction retry that ray at the original detonation coordinates and discard its invalid origin-chunk request, while retaining already committed destruction masks.

Extended logging reports an 8-by-12 equal-area-elevation/azimuth direction histogram, surface samples selected/removed and first-surface-removal delay, pressure sections skipped/inspected, targets selected/shielded, traversal and reused samples, active pressure time, deferred callback dependencies/reasons, retained original bytes, and collection/excavation active time. Surface diagnostics count grass and top opaque column samples at capture, rather than every dirt block; old checkpoints cannot reconstruct past surface counts or angular cells. Existing worker/cache, loading, suppression and timing counters remain available. Progress is limited to one report per 200 server ticks, plus existing first-removal/fallback/lifecycle events. No block or ray is individually logged. Durations and per-explosion counters persist with terrain checkpoints.

## Remaining limitations

Fallout is still the existing incremental surface-deposition system rather than a saved wind/radiation plume; “ambient contamination” has no separate supported channel in this entity. Crater ray geometry and cloud simulation remain compressed legacy gameplay systems, and EMP remains on the legacy interface. These are incremental limits, not claims of a literal nuclear simulation.
