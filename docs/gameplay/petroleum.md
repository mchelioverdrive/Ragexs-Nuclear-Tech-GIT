# Petroleum and industrial fuels

A refinery connects heat, power and separation equipment to turn crude and heavy residue into fuels, chemical feedstocks, lubricant and recoverable gas. Every stage needs room for all products. Refilling an inlet will not restart a blocked byproduct outlet.

## From deposit to refinery

Oil wells and pumpjacks extract crude and geological gas; stimulation supports higher throughput. Fresh crude ceilings are 6 L/min for a well and 24 L/min for a pumpjack, with stimulation up to 48 L/min. Route co-produced gas to recovery or disposal alongside the oil.

Heat crude before atmospheric separation. The Refinery separates hot feed into heavy oil, naphtha, light oil and petroleum gas. It accepts up to 24 L/min but needs electricity and external heat. Preheating prepares the feed for separation rather than increasing its chemical fuel energy.

## Follow the products

| Stage | Purpose | Supporting equipment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fractionation | Splits light oil into diesel/kerosene and separates supported heavier streams | Heat, powered working stages and separate outlets |
| Vacuum refining | Separates heavy residue at lower internal pressure | High-temperature heat, electrical pumping and several liquid outlets |
| Hydrotreating | Upgrades supported sulfur-bearing/cracked feeds with hydrogen | Compressed hydrogen, catalyst, heat and sour-gas handling |
| Catalytic cracking | Shifts supported feeds toward lighter hydrocarbons | Hot process heat, electricity and coke/gas outlets |
| Catalytic reforming | Makes reformate blendstock and useful hydrogen-bearing gas | Catalyst, high-temperature heat and gas recovery |
| Delayed coking | Recovers distillate, gas and petroleum coke from residues | Strong heat supply, solids handling and water outlet for wet feeds |
| Gas recovery | Sweetens sour gas, recovers LPG or separates methane/helium | Suitable feeds, power and room for every stream |

Most downstream oil stages accept up to 12 L/min; coking accepts 6 L/min. These are feed ceilings. Cracking and reforming can demand more heat than one heater supplies. Parallel heat supply can matter more than larger inlet storage.

Cracked diesel needs hydrotreating before use as normal diesel. Sulfur recovery connects **Hydrotreater → sour-gas sweetening → hydrogen sulfide → Claus recovery → sulfur**. Sweetening separates the actual mixture; it is not an equal-volume split. Keep sulfur and water/reagent returns moving.

## Fuels and lubricant

Diesel suits compression-ignition generation; kerosene supplies aviation equipment; gasoline and prepared blends have their own compatible engines. LPG is liquid fuel distinct from refinery gas. Synthesis gas is a hydrogen/carbon-monoxide mixture for synthesis and approved gas equipment. Energy content alone does not grant universal engine compatibility.

Lubricant represents mineral lubricating base oil. Start with an **Oil Well**, which uses ordinary motors and no lubricant. Heat its crude in a boiler and separate it in the atmospheric refinery. The heavy-oil outlet and recovered LPG support Chemical Reactor template **Heavy-oil Base-stock Fractionation**: propane-assisted deasphalting with recovered solvent, heating-oil and bitumen coproducts. Supply process heat and electricity and drain every outlet. This route does not require a Pumpjack or Vacuum Refinery.

The existing advanced route remains **heavy oil → Vacuum Refinery → lubricant + smear**, followed by further lubricant/bitumen recovery from smear in the Chemical Reactor or Fraction Tower. The vacuum machine needs a 400 C heat supply and electrical pumping. Pipe its small process buffers to storage to fill service containers; fractional products accumulate across batches. Old saved reformate/sour gas must drain before those outlets can accept the new products.

A filled current lubricant container supplies the hydraulic-piston crafting ingredient; the Pumpjack retains its frame, welded construction, tanks, upgraded motor and hydraulic piston. Keep a lubricant service supply for gas turbines. Reclaimed oil and heavy oils have heating, blending or recovery roles; they are not interchangeable finished motor fuels.

Diesel contains about 35.8 MJ/L at 0.833 kg/L. Electrical efficiency determines how much reaches the grid. Reforming and additives change application; octane does not multiply energy. Gas turbines can use exhaust in a [combined-cycle steam plant](power_generation.md#gas-turbines-and-combined-cycle).

## Coal and biological alternatives

Coke ovens produce coke and tar-family liquids with coproducts. Reactor routes support coal gasification/liquefaction, fermentation, digestion and oil extraction. Coal tar can feed the coker. Both fish-oil and sunflower-oil biofuel routes remain; the represented product is an oil/ethanol blend rather than transesterified biodiesel.

These alternatives still need feed, work, heat and output handling. A cold mixer blends compatible materials; it cannot replace every synthesis or upgrading stage. Use NEI to choose your pack's available route.

## A first plant

Begin with extraction, storage, heating, atmospheric refining and light-oil fractionation for diesel. Supply heaters and electrical consumers, then drain gas and heavy products. Add hydrogen and hydrotreating when feeds justify them. Expand into cracking, reforming, vacuum processing and coking when the recovered products have useful consumers. Ventilate combustion and handle sour gas as a toxic stream.
