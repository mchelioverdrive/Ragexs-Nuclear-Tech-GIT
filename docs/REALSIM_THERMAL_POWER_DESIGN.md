# RealSim thermal and power scale: feasibility design

Source review: 2026-09-27. A limited pre-RealSim cleanup now aligns direct steam-heating metadata with the staged ledger, clarifies player-facing units, and checks the stock graph when traits load. **The full RealSim balance, recipes, APIs, and save formats remain unchanged.** The existing [industrial-scale audit](INDUSTRIAL_SCALE_AUDIT.md) supplies the broader machine inventory. This document distinguishes verified current behavior from illustrative target numbers. Rates assume 20 game ticks/s and an unloaded-free, continuously supplied plant; actual sustained rates, network ports, upgrades, configs, and reactor layouts need loaded-world measurement.

## Decision

RTM can have a conserved, physically anchored steam economy without a continuous steam-table simulation. Use a small table of discrete feedwater, steam, exhaust, and coolant states with one shared heat/work ledger. **Anchor that ledger to real water enthalpy, then use one declared 1:100 gameplay energy scale** for all coupled water, steam, fuel, work, and machine ratings. Rebalance plant flow and power ratings, fuel throughput, generator output, industrial loads, storage and treatment together. A literal 1:1 implementation remains a valid higher-scale option, but the 1:100 candidate keeps useful integer fluid flows and buildable machines. The user's preference permits correcting starter generation and consumption rather than preserving today's arbitrary low power values.

Keep Forge fluid amounts and names as inventory/transport quantities, but derive **feedwater-equivalent mass** from fluid type. Steam's displayed L denotes nominal stored fluid-network quantity, not literal thermodynamic gas volume; current 100:10:1:0.1 amounts cannot all be literal gas volumes at their nominal temperatures. The present 0.5 J electrical quantum and 0.5 J/TU conversion remain exact; **do not reinterpret TU, Forge L, or old NBT values in place**. At 1:100, one displayed liquid L retains its Forge volume but carries a declared *effective thermal/fuel energy* of 1/100 real-water or real-fuel reference; it is not physically 0.01 L of water. This must be disclosed in RealSim documentation and J/L tooltips. MAGNOX/Zirnox HU remains a separate internal scale until calibrated at its coolant/steam boundary. Existing network `PU` is not bar.

## Current steam inventory and mass basis

The stock fluid definitions have one heating trait per fluid and one cooling trait per steam grade ([fluids](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/Fluids.java#L897-L945)). `FluidType.temperature` is one nominal integer per type; the fluid tank's `pressure` is a separate network `PU`, not a thermodynamic bar value ([type](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/FluidType.java#L51-L54), [tank](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/tank/FluidTank.java#L295-L325)). Water, steam volume, and mass are currently conflated in fluid amounts. The fixed 100:10:1:0.1 inventory ratios nevertheless permit a **derived**, conserved water-equivalent basis without a new NBT field:

| RTM fluid | Nominal °C | Stored amount for 1 L feedwater-equivalent | Feedwater-equivalent per stored L | Current production from cold feedwater | Current turbine step, ideal before machine efficiency |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| `FRESH_WATER`; treated `LIGHT_WATER` is a separate reactor grade | 20 by default | 1 L | 1 | Input | — |
| `STEAM` | 100 | 100 L | 0.01 | 1 L fresh → 100 L for 100 TU = **50 J** | 100 L → 1 L `SPENTSTEAM`, 100 quanta = **50 J** |
| `HOTSTEAM` | 300 | 10 L | 0.1 | 1 L fresh → 10 L for 1,600 TU = **800 J**; or 10 L steam → 1 L hot for 150 TU = 75 J | 1 L → 10 L steam, 150 quanta = **75 J** |
| `SUPERHOTSTEAM` | 450 | 1 L | 1 | 1 L fresh → 1 L super for 1,850 TU = **925 J**; or 10 L hot → 1 L super for 250 TU = 125 J | 1 L → 10 L hot, 250 quanta = **125 J** |
| `ULTRAHOTSTEAM` | 600 | 0.1 L | 10 | 10 L fresh → 1 L ultra for 18,900 TU = **9,450 J**; or 10 L super → 1 L ultra for 400 TU = 200 J | 1 L → 10 L super, 400 quanta = **200 J** |
| `SPENTSTEAM` | default 20 (no authored steam temperature) | 1 L | 1 | Turbine/steam-engine exhaust | Unpowered or powered condenser: 1 L → 1 L fresh water |

The ultrahot fraction cannot live alone in an integer `FluidTank`; one stored ultrahot L represents 10 feedwater-equivalent L. Integer batching already exists. A future flow controller must carry fractional *rate remainder* across ticks, not create fractional Forge fluid units. Generic `WATER` is raw pump output; RTM's boilers use `FRESH_WATER`, and the ReaSim RBMK inlet uses `LIGHT_WATER` ([pump and treatment inventory](INDUSTRIAL_SCALE_AUDIT.md#3-representative-system-chains), [ReaSim inlet](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/rbmk/TileEntityRBMKInlet.java#L20-L23)). `LIGHT_WATER_HOT`/`BORATED_WATER_HOT` and generic `COOLANT_HOT` are **liquid coolant loops**, each presently charged 320 TU = 160 J per L for its cold-to-hot transition, not steam grades ([fluids](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/Fluids.java#L959-L993)). Other reactor coolants (heavy water, helium, sodium, lead, salt) have independent trait values and must be calibrated by coolant family.

There is no extra stock dense-steam fluid ID in the checked RTM declarations: the four gaseous grades above implement compression by 10:1 amount changes. The [steam compressor recipes](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/recipes/CompressorRecipes.java#L22-L24) use 1,000 L input → 100 L output at each grade, conserving the **derived water equivalent**, but they add a higher-energy steam grade using electricity; their energy bill must be included in every loop. Steam is also used/produced in chemistry and as pump or production-machine process fluid. Those recipes are **external material/heat sources or sinks**, not automatically closed Rankine-cycle steps ([steam recipe search](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/recipes/ChemplantRecipes.java)).

## Reproduced current heat/work ledger and reachability

This pre-RealSim cleanup changed only the three unused direct fresh-water heating entries, player-facing wording, and a stock-trait diagnostic. It did not rebalance turbines, boilers, reactors, fuels, industrial loads, transport, storage, fluid amounts, or throughput. The future RealSim pass will replace the current tiny absolute energy scale with a physically anchored scaled ledger.

Per **one** feedwater-equivalent liter, expanding `SUPERHOTSTEAM` through three appropriate turbines credits 250 + 10×150 + 100 = **1,850 electrical quanta = 925 J** at ideal trait efficiency. A standard train at its default 85% yields about **786 J**, and an industrial train at 100% yields **925 J**. The direct fresh-water → superhot trait now charges **1,850 TU = 925 J**, equal to staged heat and ideal downstream work. Hot steam direct charges **1,600 TU = 800 J** per feedwater-equivalent L. For ultrahot, the existing 10-feedwater-L → 1-stored-L batch now charges **18,900 TU = 9,450 J**, equal to four-stage ideal recovery: 400 + 10×250 + 100×150 + 10×100 quanta. These direct entries previously charged only 500, 250, and 900 TU respectively; those contradictions were unused by stock heaters.

**Reachability changes the conclusion.** Every built-in heating call site inspected uses `FT_Heatable.getFirstStep()`; later direct-water steps are serialized but are **not selected by the stock boiler, heat exchanger, PWR/Watz/ITER/ICF/Hephaestus, or RBMK heater** ([trait](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/trait/FT_Heatable.java#L49-L55), [boiler](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityHeatBoilerBase.java#L199-L226)). The accessible first-step route charges 50 J water → ordinary steam, then 750 J to reheat 100 L steam → 10 L hot, then 125 J to reheat 10 L hot → 1 L super: **925 J heat for 925 J ideal turbine work**. The extra ultrahot reheat is 200 J per 10 feedwater L, likewise matched by 200 J expansion work. With the [electric heater](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityHeaterElectric.java#L201-L216) converting about 50% of electric input to stored TU, and turbine efficiency at 85%/100%, the stock *electric heater → stock staged boiler/reheater → turbine* loop returns at most **42.5%/50%** of its electric input before condenser/pump costs. It is **not a demonstrated electrical perpetual-motion loop**. The default direct-route metadata now agrees with the staged ledger. Custom JSON trait overrides can still introduce inconsistent values; a registration-time warning checks the known stock graph. The later RealSim design will replace the tiny absolute scale with a physically anchored scaled ledger.

The [compressor](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityMachineCompressor.java#L57-L59) currently draws 25 kW for a 100-tick default steam recipe: **125 kJ per batch** before upgrades. This exceeds its current grade-step work credit. Under the illustrative new ledger below, the large `SUPERHOTSTEAM` → `ULTRAHOTSTEAM` batch would gain much more enthalpy than 125 kJ, so compressor energy and/or external heat must be changed with the steam table. Powered condensation costs 10 quanta = 5 J per spent-steam L; the unpowered condenser instead rejects heat to the environment with no explicit sink rating ([condenser](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityCondenser.java#L99-L123), [powered](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityCondenserPowered.java#L27-L58)). Electric pumping and feedwater treatment are additional parasitic loads.

No universal closed-loop proof is possible from static source alone: reactor core `heat`/HU and sun input are not universally joules, custom JSON traits and recipes can alter conversions, and third-party producers can insert steam. The verified statement is narrower: **stock reachable staged steam traits plus electric heater do not gain electricity** at default efficiencies; **the corrected default direct-water traits no longer undercharge ideal turbine work**. The registration-time check warns if loaded stock traits contradict this ledger, but it does not validate arbitrary custom fluids, recipes, or all external heat sources. A later implementation must enumerate every steam-producing source and check cycles after each config/recipe load.

## Upstream NTM and NTM: Space comparison

Current [HBM/NTM master fluid definitions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HbmMods/Hbm-s-Nuclear-Tech-GIT/master/src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/Fluids.java#L604-L619) charge `WATER` 200/220/238 TU for ordinary/hot/superhot steam per feedwater L, and 2,500 TU for 10 L → 1 L ultrahot. Turbine steps credit 200/2/18/120 quanta respectively for ordinary/hot/superhot/ultrahot input operations. Because TU and electrical quantum are each 0.5 J in RTM's interpretation, the upstream **direct** ideal ledgers close exactly: 100 J ordinary; 100 + 10×1 = **110 J** hot; 110 + 9 = **119 J** superhot; 10×119 + 60 = **1,250 J** for 10 L → ultrahot. That is conservation by authored table, not physically realistic water enthalpy: ordinary steam is still ~26,000× below a literal 20-to-100 °C boil. Upstream uses the same 100:10:1:0.1 compression ratios.

The checked local `upstream/space-travel-twopointfive` NTM: Space ref (commit `64b641309`, 2026-09-21) carries those same water costs and steam turbine work values in `Fluids.java`; its standard/industrial turbines retain 85%/100% machine defaults, the 6,000-stored-steam-L/t standard cap and 20%-of-current-inlet industrial behavior. This is a dated local ref, not proof that an un-fetched remote has no newer commit. RTM retained those inherited machine defaults but introduced `FRESH_WATER`/reactor-grade water and changed steam heat/work traits. **Preserve upstream's invariant that every direct and staged route agrees with one ledger; do not restore its small absolute numbers.** Neither inspected upstream variant supplies a pressure-aware thermodynamic state model or solves RTM's reactor boundary calibration.

NTM: Space's `TileEntityHeatBoiler` also uses `getFirstStep()`, charges `entry.heatReq / BOILER efficiency` for each operation and stores heat as `int` with a 3,200,000-unit default maximum (checked local ref, lines 201–229 and 291–300). RTM's shared boiler base still selects the first step and retains an integer heat store, but its boiler conversion tests the efficiency as an enable flag without dividing heat cost by it. That inherited-vs-RTM distinction matters when restoring one thermal ledger; copy neither implementation's factor blindly.

## Physical reference and the limits of temperature labels

[NIST's IAPWS tables](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir5078.pdf) give about **83.9 kJ/kg** liquid-water enthalpy at 20 °C and, at 100 °C/0.101 MPa, **419.2 kJ/kg** saturated liquid, **2,675.6 kJ/kg** saturated vapor, and **2,256.4 kJ/kg** latent heat. The 20-to-100 °C sensible increment implies water specific heat averaging about **4.19 kJ/(kg·K)**. Thus a 20 °C liquid liter to 100 °C saturated steam needs roughly **2.59 MJ/kg** (about 2.59 MJ per real liquid liter). At a representative **9 MPa and 450 °C**, the same NIST table gives roughly **3.26 MJ/kg** superheated-steam enthalpy. These values depend on both temperature **and pressure**; NIST's steam tables also give vapor specific volume about **1,672 L/kg** at 100 °C/0.101 MPa. RTM's 100 stored steam L per liquid-water L therefore cannot simultaneously be literal ambient-pressure gas volume, literal 1 kg, and 100 °C saturation. Use the stored steam L as a **nominal transport amount**; show feedwater-equivalent mass/flow when a physical claim matters.

[DOE's industrial steam survey](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/manufacturing/tech_assistance/pdfs/steam_survey_guide.pdf) defines turbine isentropic efficiency from the actual versus ideal enthalpy drop, and uses 85% boiler efficiency in an example. [DOE's turbine examples](https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/sites/default/files/attachments/CHP_Steam_Turbines.pdf) show roughly 60–70% isentropic efficiency for 250–500 kW backpressure units and 80–85% for a 25 MW unit; they distinguish process backpressure exhaust from a condensing turbine's vacuum exhaust. A [DOE-hosted power-cycle reference](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/6565025) illustrates a 16.5 MPa/540 °C inlet, 5.5 kPa condenser, 82–86% stage efficiencies and 90% steam-generator efficiency. These are *turbine-stage* and boiler efficiencies, not whole plant thermal-to-electric efficiencies; such a high-pressure cycle is a reference, **not** an RTM steam-grade mapping. RTM's configurable 85%/100% turbine multipliers can remain **game machine factors** applied to a conservative extractable-work budget; 100% must not be displayed as 100% heat-to-electric conversion. Fuel/boiler efficiency, condenser rejection, feedwater preheat, exhaust use, and auxiliary power remain separate. Temperature class may remain discrete; a per-tick equation of state, quality field, continuous pressure solver, and full steam tables are not required.

The liquid-water **heat-capacity error is distinct from latent heat**. At 1:100, the NIST-derived reference is about **41.9 J/(displayed L·K) effective**, so a defined 100 °C rise requires about **4.19 kJ/L**. The present generic, light-water and borated-water cold→hot traits charge only **160 J/L** without a declared inlet/outlet temperature difference—roughly **26× below** that scaled 100 °C example. Literal 1:1 water would require about **419 kJ/L** over the same rise. These are examples, not automatic new trait values: RTM must assign each liquid coolant grade an actual temperature interval and effective heat capacity before setting its J/L transition. Steam's 25.9-kJ/L scaled boiling charge includes the much larger latent heat and cannot be inferred from liquid specific heat alone. CO₂, helium, oils, molten salt and fantasy coolants need separate state data; Zirnox HU is not water specific heat.

## Three scaling choices

Baseline example: current standard turbine at its 6,000 ordinary-steam L/t cap requires **60 feedwater-equivalent L/t**. Existing ordinary steam charges 50 J/L, so the full thermal input is **60 kW**, for **51 kW** gross standard-turbine output. Candidate figures below use 2.59 MJ/kg physical boiling demand and an **illustrative** 20% overall superheated-cycle electrical fraction; actual turbine-stage work is specified separately below. Retaining existing flow with literal heat would create a huge plant; **reducing and explicitly rating flow** is the first balancing lever, followed by scaling loads and fuel use. It is acceptable for starter generators and consumers to rise together.

| Model | Heat per displayed liquid-water L | At unchanged 60 L/t feed | Effect on plant and logistics | Assessment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| **A. Literal**: 1 liquid L ≈ 1 kg | ~2.59 MJ ordinary steam | ~3.1 GW thermal; order 0.6 GW electric at 20% | Existing 51 kW turbine and kW consumers become irrelevant if flow is untouched. A 0.5 MW plant at 20% needs only ~0.05 L/t feed; smaller machines need cross-tick batching. Existing water tanks last much longer, while high-volume steam headers and large plants remain relevant. | Physically clean, but requires much smaller per-tick flows and far larger ratings than today's hardware. Retain as an explicit comparison and possible later high-scale mode. |
| **B. Uniform energy compression, 1:10** | ~259 kJ | ~311 MW thermal | Reduces power and thermal storage targets by 10×; each displayed liquid L carries one tenth of the real-liter reference heat. | Higher-power option if 1:100 proves too weak for late tiers. |
| **B. Uniform compression, 1:100** | ~25.9 kJ | ~31 MW thermal | Allows 1–4 L/t starter/standard feed and ~40 L/t industrial feed with integer flows; preserves visible water, steam and fuel movement. Relationships come from real reference enthalpies with one fixed energy factor. | **Recommended initial RealSim target**, subject to the acceptance gates below. |
| **B. Uniform compression, 1:1,000** | ~2.59 kJ | ~3.1 MW thermal | Still 52× today's 50-J/L boiling input, but gives relatively weak plant output at buildable flows. | Keep only as a lower-power fallback. |
| **C. Explicit water-equivalent accounting** | Chosen by A or B | Steam inventory ratios remain 100:10:1:0.1 | This is a *representation architecture*, not another energy factor. It prevents 100 L steam from being treated as 100 L liquid mass and permits mass-rated turbines independent of grade. | **Adopt with B 1:100** and retain for any later scale factor. |

At fixed **4 L/t standard** and **40 L/t industrial** superhot feed, with the 85%/100% machine factors and the same provisional 550-kJ/L *physical-reference* full expansion budget, the model choices give:

| Energy scale | Standard heat / gross electric | Industrial heat / gross electric | Consequence for the same fluid build |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Literal 1:1 | 254.4 MWth / 37.4 MWe | 2.544 GWth / 440 MWe | Steam headers, treatment and water tank *durations* stay fixed; heat sources, coolant, batteries and loads need enormous absolute ratings. |
| Uniform 1:10 | 25.44 MWth / 3.74 MWe | 254.4 MWth / 44 MWe | Strong reactor and industry tiers; starter fuel/steam hardware still needs high thermal transfer. |
| **Uniform 1:100** | **2.544 MWth / 0.374 MWe** | **25.44 MWth / 4.4 MWe** | Water, steam and fuel logistics remain visible, while MW-class industry is reachable. |
| Uniform 1:1,000 | 0.2544 MWth / 0.0374 MWe | 2.544 MWth / 0.44 MWe | A plant this size has little room for the large-industrial loads sought by RealSim. |

Changing the energy factor alone cannot change stored L, pipe L/t, condenser water return, or treatment L/t. It **does** change heat-source and exchanger W, electric output, coolant heat rejection W, fuel energy per L, and battery runtime at a given load. If cooling water's effective heat capacity uses the same factor, its circulation L/t for a chosen temperature rise is approximately scale-invariant. That is why flow ratings and loads still require an independent balance decision.

With 1:100, display **scaled J per liquid-water L**, J/W, and feedwater-equivalent L/s. Do not call this literal measured water heat capacity or imply that displayed W is an unscaled physical plant rating. Steam's stored L remains a **nominal fluid-network amount**, so show both stored steam L and water-equivalent flow in plant panels. Do not label a conversion enthalpy as `J/(L·K)` or as measured water specific heat. The `FT_Heatable`/`FT_Coolable` tooltips now show heat required or recoverable transition energy **per stored fluid-network L** for the first heating step or one cooling step. This is discrete transition energy, not specific heat or energy per feedwater-equivalent L for compressed grades. Keep `°C` as nominal grade labels until temperature/pressure pairs are assigned. Existing fluid-network `PU` must remain `PU`, not be relabeled bar.

## Illustrative reference and scaled discrete-state ledger (not a balance patch)

The following rounded values are **provisional engineering design points** based on NIST's physical enthalpy scale, not a finalized set of consistent pressure/temperature states. In particular, the steam-grade pressure classes and isentropic exits require a state-table pass before implementation. They preserve the large vaporization increment and make stage work no greater than each state's enthalpy drop. Reference is 20 °C liquid feedwater = 0. All entries are per **1 L liquid feedwater-equivalent ≈ 1 kg**.

| State | State enthalpy, J | Next expansion | Maximum credited ideal work, J | Residual heat/enthalpy treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| Cold feedwater | 0 | — | — | Reference; preheating is an optional separate input. |
| Hot feedwater | 334,000 | — | — | Sensible heat from ~20 to 100 °C. |
| Ordinary steam | 2,590,000 | → spent steam | 150,000 | Spent steam remains around 2.4 MJ; condenser rejects it, rather than converting it to electricity. |
| Hot steam | 2,980,000 | → ordinary steam | 250,000 | 390 kJ stage enthalpy gap; at least 140 kJ remains as waste/irreversibility before machine-efficiency losses. |
| Superhot steam | 3,180,000 | → hot steam | 150,000 | 200 kJ gap; 50 kJ unconverted. |
| Ultrahot steam | 3,500,000 | → superhot steam | 200,000 per feedwater-equivalent L | One stored ultrahot L is **10** feedwater-equivalent L, so it carries 2 MJ ideal stage work. |
| Spent/low-pressure steam | 2,400,000 | → condensate | 0 | Condenser rejects roughly 2.4 MJ/feedwater L. |

For the recommended 1:100 target, divide **every heat and work entry in the table by 100**, without changing any fluid amount. Charge `h(out) − h(in)` times **feedwater-equivalent amount**: 1 L fresh → ordinary needs **25.9 kJ**; direct fresh → superhot needs **31.8 kJ**; staged 100 L ordinary → 10 L hot needs **3.9 kJ**; 10 L hot → 1 L super needs **2.0 kJ**. A full superhot train yields at most **5.5 kJ electrical per feedwater L** at 100% machine factor, or **4.675 kJ** at 85%, from 31.8 kJ heat input. The balance exits in exhaust, cooling, and stage losses. Ordinary-only yields 1.5 kJ ideal from 25.9 kJ heat. Compressor work must be at least the enthalpy it adds (less any explicit external heat); a 1,000 L superhot → 100 L ultrahot batch represents 1,000 feedwater-equivalent L and adds **3.2 MJ** on this scale, still far above its current 125 kJ electric bill. Such a recipe must be retuned or given an explicit heat input before this model can be enabled. At literal 1:1, the corresponding batch would add 320 MJ.

An implementable representation is a shared steam-state table keyed by existing `FluidType` ID, with rational water-equivalent numerator/denominator and `long` energy per conversion batch. Stock `FluidTank` and `FluidStack` integers remain unchanged. For arbitrary addon fluids without a registered state, retain legacy trait behavior through a documented compatibility boundary; do **not** invent a density or thermodynamic pressure. New stage math and the compressor/boiler selectors must use one table so the same state cannot carry contradictory authored heat and work values.

## Hardware implications and representative proposed plants

The present [standard turbine](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityMachineTurbine.java#L73-L83) limits **stored steam L/t** to 6,000, so compact superhot steam can represent 100× the feedwater mass of ordinary steam at the same inlet cap. The [industrial turbine](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityMachineLargeTurbine.java#L221-L231) processes 20% of its current inlet per tick and has no independent rated mass flow. Both use `maxPower` as an *energy buffer*, not a watt rating. The steam engine has a 2,000 L inlet/20 L exhaust and no separate mass-flow nameplate ([engine](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntitySteamEngine.java#L55-L67)); Leviathan likewise uses large buffers and default 85% efficiency, plus trip valves ([Leviathan](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityChungus.java#L72-L80), [safety](steam-turbines.md)). Proposed first ratings at **1:100** are **1 feedwater-equivalent L/t** for a starter steam engine, **4 L/t** for a standard turbine, and **40 L/t** for an industrial turbine, each with a separate generator W cap. A **train** below means three turbine machines in series—superhot→hot, hot→ordinary, and ordinary→spent—each passing the same feedwater-equivalent flow. This gives **25.5 kW** ordinary gross for the engine, **0.102 MW ordinary / 0.374 MW superhot** gross for a standard train at 85%, and **1.2 MW ordinary / 4.4 MW superhot** gross for an industrial train at 100%. Leviathan needs its own class above industrial; its one-billion-L tanks are not a rating. Exact targets must follow real build tests.

The [standard heat boiler](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityHeatBoiler.java#L26-L36) has 16,000 L feed, 1.6 million L ordinary steam, and a 3.2-million-TU (1.6 MJ) thermal buffer; the [industrial boiler](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityHeatBoilerIndustrial.java#L22-L35) has 64,000 L feed, 6.4 million L steam, and 6.4 MJ heat storage. Both convert all available operations in a tick, limited by buffer heat, feed, and outlet room, without an independent thermal W or steam mass-flow nameplate ([conversion](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityHeatBoilerBase.java#L179-L226)). At the 1:100 target, the present standard heat buffer could hold enough heat for about **62 L of fresh-water boiling**, but the boiler still needs a **2.54 MWth** input and transfer rating to sustain the proposed standard superhot plant. The electric heater yields about 1 kW heat from 2 kW electricity at base setting and stores 250 kJ, so it is a **starter/process heater**, not a future MW boiler source. Oil/firebox/solar sources and heat exchangers also need explicit heat-generation and transfer ratings. The [solar boiler](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntitySolarBoiler.java#L108-L131) hardcodes `thermalInput / 50` rather than consulting the fluid trait. The [coolant exchanger](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/TileEntityHeaterHeatex.java#L165-L180) has an operation cap and int heat buffer, but its sustainable MWth depends on coolant, sink, and ports. The existing `HeatingType.BOILER` trait efficiency is only checked as enabled by the standard boiler; it is not applied as a thermal-efficiency multiplier in its conversion math.

**Candidate 1:100 plant calculations:** `Qth = 20 × feedwater-equivalent L/t × scaled state input J/L`; `gross MWe = 20 × L/t × scaled sum(stage ideal work J/L) × machine factor / 10^6`. The auxiliary columns below are **illustrative envelopes**, not measured machine totals. They include current condenser draw (5 J per spent L), a current 10 kW electric water pump if used, and stated assumed future coolant/control/treatment loads. In a recirculating loop, treatment is mainly startup/makeup, not the whole steam flow. Current default batteries/tanks are cited only to show duration, not as proposed ratings. On the physical reference scale, multiply the listed gross heat/work by 100 **only after** deciding whether flow and auxiliaries should remain the same.

The **literal 1:1 reference at the same listed water flows** would multiply the steam heat and gross electric columns by 100: the midgame superhot plant would be **254 MWth / 37.4 MWe**, Zirnox at its current 55-L/t ceiling would imply **3.50 GWth / 605 MWe**, and the 200-L/t RBMK/PWR design case would imply **12.72 GWth / 2.2 GWe**. Those are arithmetic comparisons, **not established reactor outputs**. A literal mode would instead need substantially lower rated flows, separate fuel and coolant calibration, and much larger machine buffers. The 1:100 option keeps the water/steam logistics visible while still moving ordinary boiling from today's 50 J/L to **25,900 J/L**, a **518×** correction tied to a real reference value.

Fuel and cooling create useful infrastructure even with condensate return. If the midgame 2.544-MWth plant burns candidate 350-kJ/L diesel at an **assumed 85% boiler fuel-to-steam efficiency**, it needs about **0.428 fuel L/t**; an existing 4,000 L fuel buffer would last roughly **7.8 minutes** at steady output. The condenser must reject about **24 kJ per feedwater-equivalent L**, or **1.92 MWth** for that plant and **192 MWth** for the 400-L/t late station, before additional stage losses. If a separate circulating-water loop accepts a 10 °C rise and uses the same 1:100 water-heat scale (about 41.9 J/(L·K) effective), its theoretical minimum flow is about **229 L/t** for the midgame condenser and **22,900 L/t** for the late condenser. Those are candidate heat-sink ratings, not current RTM requirements; actual exchanger approach, pump power, ambient heat rejection, and pipe behavior can increase them. They show why cooling-water **circulation** and heat rejection matter more than perpetual fresh-water makeup. The existing nominal 10,000-L/t groundwater pump cannot by itself establish any closed-loop cooling rate or supply high-grade treated feedwater.

**Storage correction:** a 256,000 L general tank holds 256,000 L liquid water (~5.3 min at 40 L/t), but if filled with ordinary steam it contains only **2,560 feedwater-equivalent L**, or **64 ticks = 3.2 s** at a 40-L/t industrial train. For the 400-L/t late design case it lasts 6.4 ticks. The candidate table's late header must therefore use several steam drums or deliberate continuous delivery. At 4.4 MWe, the present industrial turbine 50 MJ electrical buffer lasts ~11.4 s; a 25 MJ lithium battery lasts ~5.7 s at that entire load. The ordinary 0.5 MJ battery cannot provide that full load at any duration: its existing discharge rating is only about 16.7 kW. Existing `FluidTank` NBT persists both amount **and max capacity**, so changing defaults alone will not retrofit old tanks ([tank NBT](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/tank/FluidTank.java#L304-L325)).

The [industrial-scale audit](INDUSTRIAL_SCALE_AUDIT.md#2-subsystem-analysis) finds 0.5–2 kW starter tools, 25 kW compressor, 100 kW mining laser, base 10 kW large arc furnace (5× per speed tier), 50 W refinery auxiliary at 100 L/t throughput, and only about 8 kW for eight active industrial-fabrication lanes. These **are current values, not protected targets**. Candidate *family envelopes*, not final per-recipe numbers: hand/first electric tools 1–10 kW; powered workshop and basic fabrication/chemistry 5–50 kW; compressor/electrolysis 0.1–1 MW depending throughput; oil refining and pumping 0.1–2 MW electrical **plus separate process heat**; large arc/foundry/fabrication 0.5–10 MW with lane, speed and material scaling; late mining/defense/high-energy processing 1–30 MW. The Industrial Fabrication Machine already charges per active lane; preserve that accounting and adjust its lane rate if needed. A 43-MWe station should have multiple MW consumers and appropriate operating costs, while first power remains buildable with wood or other pre-fuel-processing sources.

Generation must follow the **same declared fuel-energy scale**. Actual diesel is roughly [35 MJ/L](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2014/03/f14/20110512135206-0.pdf); the 1:100 game ledger would be about **350 kJ per displayed L** before engine efficiency. For illustration, a 100 kW starter diesel generator at 30% electrical efficiency would burn about **0.048 L/t averaged**, or one whole Forge L every ~21 ticks; a 4,000 L fuel tank would last about **70 minutes** at that load. Integer batching or a fractional rate remainder is required; retaining today's 1 L/t diesel burn with this fuel energy would yield **2.1 MW** and trivialize midgame. A first wood generator around 10–20 kW and a workshop that grows from today's 4.5 kW to perhaps 30–50 kW are *provisional* progression targets, not new defaults. They allow the pre-diesel bootstrap while making the diesel and steam tiers useful. Fuel grade efficiencies, pollution and recipe access must be rechecked before setting exact values; fantasy fuels may remain explicitly abstracted.

Energy storage, transfer rating, and fluid capacity must be separate. Current batteries discharge at capacity/600 quanta/t and charge at capacity/200, tying watts to joules; review ratings by tier before changing capacity ([battery](../src/main/java/com/hbm/tileentity/machine/storage/TileEntityMachineBattery.java#L317-L322)). Plain MK2 power cables have no intrinsic W cap, generic MK2 fluid endpoints default to a 1-billion-L/t receiver fallback, and actual pipe/port topology may impose other limits ([power net](../src/main/java/api/hbm/energymk2/PowerNetMK2.java#L235-L253), [fluid net](../src/main/java/api/hbm/fluidmk2/FluidNetMK2.java#L262)). Do not add a cosmetic cable/pipe nameplate until loaded-world throughput is measured. For the design flows, measure 100/400/4,000 stored ordinary-steam L/t headers for the 1/4/40-L/t steam tiers, plus the 40,000-L/t late station header and grade-specific ports; then rate pipe, pump, condenser, and tank ports. A 10,000-L/t electric groundwater pump is far above the candidate boiler feeds but is raw-water production, not guaranteed treatment or recirculation. At the proposed 400-L/t late return, one existing ~9-L/t water-treatment line could only support roughly 2% of an **open** loop; high condensate return is therefore necessary, with makeup and blowdown kept explicit.

## Architecture, compatibility, and implementation order

1. **Freeze a balance ledger and data contract.** Enumerate stock and JSON steam-producing recipes, compressor steps, fluid conversions, electric heaters, and addon boundaries. Declare water-equivalent ratios and integer rounding. Specify physical reference states (temperature **and** pressure class) only where supported; validate `h_out ≥ h_in + supplied heat` and `work ≤ h_in − h_out` for every applicable transition. Distinguish external fuel, solar, reactor fission/fusion, and chemical enthalpy sources from closed electric loops.
2. **Introduce shared discrete steam-state accounting.** A new central table can derive water equivalent and J from existing `FluidType` and amount while preserving fluid IDs, Forge quantities, NBT and packet amounts. Convert stock boiler/reheater, compressor, turbine/engine, condenser, and relevant recipes to the table in one coherent migration. Keep `FT_Heatable`/`FT_Coolable` as external legacy adapters only if stock paths no longer maintain a second contradictory steam ledger. Do not relabel `PU` as bar. Normalize UI labels and add MWth/MWe and water-equivalent flow where useful.
3. **Separate thermal stores, heat-transfer W, and mass-flow L/t.** Standard/industrial boilers, heaters, oil/firebox/solar heat, and exchangers must have independent ratings. The shared operation planner should bound one tick by inlet, outlet, heat available, thermal transfer limit, and mass-flow limit; use `long` intermediates and checked operations. Keep existing safety, full-buffer pause, exhaust blockage/rupture, and Leviathan trip behavior.
4. **Calibrate reactor boundaries one family at a time.** Instrument or measure steady exported coolant/steam heat, fuel burn, temperature and auxiliary power. Convert **output boundaries**, not old core HU or temperature storage. Start with a non-nuclear boiler and Zirnox; then ReaSim RBMK, PWR, salt, Watz, and finally fusion. For ITER, first reconcile packet input, shield capture and coolant MWth before choosing electrical output.
5. **Balance generation, demand, storage and transport by tier.** Rebuild the pre-diesel bootstrap at the new scale, then raise diesel and other fuel generators against the declared chemical-energy ledger. Raise workshop and heavy process draw by machine family and recipe throughput, rather than preserving existing starter numbers or multiplying everything blindly. Set battery W ratings and heat-buffer seconds independently. Verify actual cable, pipe, port and condenser sustained throughput before adding rated tiers. Preserve meaningful boiler/fuel/turbine efficiencies unless evidence calls for a change.
6. **Migrate worlds and configs deliberately.** Existing integer `FluidTank` amount, max, type and `PU` are persisted; energy quanta use long NBT; several thermal buffers are still int TU/heat (`IHeatSource`). A new thermal scale may need long buffers and versioned NBT/config migration, because old heat values cannot silently become new J values. Changes to default tank capacity alone do not update saved caps. Keep fluid ID, Forge fluid compatibility and recipe serialization stable where possible. Added state metadata should be derived from type; only true new fractional accumulators or rated settings need versioned storage. Document addon behavior, optionally expose a stable state-table API, and keep unknown-fluid fallback honest.

**What changes by data versus code:** water/steam enthalpy, basic grade volumes, fuel budgets and per-family machine loads are data; first-step-only selectors, grade-independent turbine mass caps, unbounded boiler heat-to-steam operations, solar hardcoding, integer heat-buffer limits, and reactor output-boundary accounting require code changes. A new continuous thermodynamic solver, Forge fluid rewrite, fluid amount rescale, universal reactor-core rewrite, or broad machine rewrite is **not required**. This is still a multi-subsystem migration; a display-only conversion cannot meet the physical ledger goal.

**Values and contracts to retain initially:** 20 ticks/s; the exact 0.5 J electrical quantum and 0.5 J/TU unit meaning; fluid IDs and numeric Forge amounts; existing world tank contents and packet amounts; steam inventory ratios until a separately justified transport redesign; initial 85%/100% turbine machine factors; existing reactor safety and core-control behavior; and the natural-uranium/first-power production dependency chain. Rated watts, per-operation heat/work, fuel J/L, burn rates, machine draws, battery discharge limits, and heat buffers are deliberately **open to rebalance**. Where a persisted maximum or heat store changes, migrate it explicitly rather than silently assigning a new interpretation to an old NBT integer.

## Acceptance gates and known limitations

- **Conservation:** For every stock steam path and loaded config, graph the state transitions by feedwater-equivalent amount; no cycle with no declared external fuel, solar, reactor, chemistry, or electricity source can produce net electricity or net high-grade steam. Test compressor-assisted, electric-heater, direct-water and multistage routes separately. Reject impossible `efficiency > 1` values or label them explicit cheat settings.
- **Plant behavior:** In a loaded world, each canonical plant reaches sustained inlet, exhaust, condenser, coolant and power flow for at least several buffer durations, with no overflow, oscillation, phantom fluid, or unsafe bypass. Measure starter bootstrap, reactor control transients, blocked ports, overpressure, and chunk reload.
- **Progression:** One or two **rebalanced** starter generators can operate first powered tools; early steam is optional/useful; first natural-uranium reactor and complete cooling/return route are buildable before requiring reactor-only materials. Midgame power choices and late multi-MW loads remain distinct ([RealSim progression](REALSIM_PRODUCTION.md#current-progression-anchor)).
- **Compatibility:** Existing worlds preserve fluid amounts/types and energy quanta; thermal buffers and saved maxima receive explicit migration if changed. External Forge fluids and add-ons either use a documented state registration or retain legacy behavior without claiming physical enthalpy. Config/script overrides do not silently invert energy accounting.
- **UI:** J/W are internally exact; thermal input and electric output are separately labeled MWth/MWe; steam transport L and feedwater-equivalent mass/flow are both visible where needed; `J/(L·K)` appears only for an actual specific-heat model. `PU` stays a network pressure unit unless calibrated to bar.

**Unresolved before choosing final targets:** real sustained heat output from firebox/oil/solar systems; fuel chemistry versus displayed J/L; each reactor's steady fuel-to-coolant heat and HU bridge; pressure/temperature classes for steam grades; steam-recipe and addon graph; coolant exchanger and condenser rejection rates; pipe/port bottlenecks under chunk loading; late factory utilization and upgrade mix; tank capacity migration policy. These are measurement/design gates, not reasons to abandon the discrete-state approach.
