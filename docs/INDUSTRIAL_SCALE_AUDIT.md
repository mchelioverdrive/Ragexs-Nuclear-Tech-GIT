# RTM industrial power, heat, fluid, and consumption audit

Source audit: 2026-09-26. **Recommendation only; no balance values or save formats were changed.** Figures below use the checked-in defaults, no upgrades, 20 ticks/s, and continuously available inputs and outputs unless stated. Configurable machines, gamerules, recipes, fuel grades, world deposits, and block layout can change realized rates. A machine's buffer capacity is not its continuous output rating. No Minecraft run or loaded-world measurement was performed.

## Method and reference scale

Electrical storage uses half-joule energy quanta: 1 quantum = 0.5 J and 1 quantum/t = 10 W. The thermal fluid traits use 1 TU = 0.5 J and 1 TU/t = 10 W. One internal fluid unit is displayed as 1 L; a Minecraft bucket is 1,000 L. These are *unit conversions*, not evidence that independently authored HBM fuel, reactor, and steam models conserve physical energy. MAGNOX/Zirnox HU is kept separate. All throughput below is in game time, not wall time under server lag. See [energy units](../src/main/java/api/hbm/energymk2/EnergyUnits.java), [fluid traits](../src/main/java/com/hbm/inventory/fluid/Fluids.java#L897-L975), and [steam turbine guide](steam-turbines.md).

Classification: **Realistic** means the number can be physically close without hurting play; **Scaled realistic** preserves hierarchy and order of magnitude with playable compression; **Abstracted** retains an intentional game mechanism, but must still obey surrounding system constraints. Engineering comparison is approximate: DOE's [industrial motor guide](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2014/04/f15/amo_motors_guidebook_web.pdf) places substantial motors in the tens to hundreds of horsepower, and DOE's [steam-system guidance](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/best-management-practice-8-steam-boiler-systems) emphasizes condensate return, makeup water, and blowdown. Those references guide *relative scale*; RTM does not need literal fuel density, plant size, or water withdrawal. The later [RealSim thermal and power design](REALSIM_THERMAL_POWER_DESIGN.md) revises this audit's recommendation to preserve starter power values: a coordinated generator and consumer rebalance is now in scope for a future implementation.

The numerical SI scale is intentionally far below literal thermodynamics: RTM's ordinary-steam recipe charges **50 J/L** water, while vaporizing a liter of water at atmospheric pressure alone takes approximately **2.26 MJ** ([DOE steam tip sheet](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/manufacturing/tech_assistance/pdfs/steam_26a.pdf)), about **45,000×** more. RTM's default diesel flammable trait is roughly **275 J/L**, versus about **35 MJ/L** for actual diesel ([DOE fuel comparison](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2014/03/f14/20110512135206-0.pdf)). These are gameplay-compressed energy budgets; multiplying all joules by a physical correction factor would destroy buildability. The stronger requirement is that RTM's *own* heat, fuel and electrical conversion ledgers reconcile.

**Steam trait cleanup:** The earlier direct fresh-water → superhot entry charged only 500 TU = 250 J for a state with 1,850 quanta = 925 J of ideal downstream turbine work. The 3.15–3.7× comparison described a **formerly inconsistent, unreachable direct route**, not a stock closed-loop exploit: built-in heaters select only `getFirstStep()`, and staged heating already cost 1,850 TU per feedwater-equivalent L. Direct hot and ultrahot entries were also inconsistent. The direct entries now match the staged totals: 1,600 TU per feedwater L to hot, 1,850 TU per feedwater L to superhot, and 18,900 TU per 10-feedwater-L batch to ultrahot. A registration-time diagnostic warns about contradictions in the loaded stock graph, including custom trait overrides. The stock turbine/boiler/reactor balance is unchanged; see the [RealSim design](REALSIM_THERMAL_POWER_DESIGN.md#reproduced-current-heatwork-ledger-and-reachability).

In physical terms, a 0.5–2 kW powered workshop is credible for small tools; 25 kW is a roughly 34 hp compressor motor; 51 kW is a modest generator, whereas an RTM industrial turbine at its temporary 1.024 MW inlet ceiling is a small industrial power unit. A 100 L/t refinery processes **2 m³/s**, so 50 W of auxiliary electricity cannot credibly represent its pumps, controls and drives even if process heat is separately supplied. A 10,000 L/t water pump moves **200 m³/s** for 10 kW, which is a game abstraction unless lift and pressure are negligible. A 256,000 L tank is **256 m³**, useful but only two minutes of full refinery feed. These comparisons are inferences from RTM's own rates and basic unit arithmetic, not proposed literal targets.

## 4. Proposed corrections, gameplay effect and priority

| Priority | Recommendation / implementation unit | Why and gameplay effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High (future RealSim)** | **Physically anchored steam ledger by steam-grade family.** Publish heat input, recoverable work, nominal stored-fluid ratios and condenser return per feedwater-equivalent liter. Preserve existing 85%/100% machine factors unless independent testing justifies changing them. | The former 3.15–3.7× direct-route inconsistency has been corrected in default metadata; stock staged heating was already balanced before machine losses. A physically anchored scaled ledger still requires coordinated plant and load scaling. |
| **High** | **Industrial consumer family scaling**, focused on refinery auxiliaries, large arc/foundry, large chemical/fabrication lines and actual per-lane work. A provisional **3–30×** family range was this audit's initial envelope; the later [RealSim design](REALSIM_THERMAL_POWER_DESIGN.md#reactor-thermal-boundary-loads-and-networks) also permits coordinated starter generator and machine increases. | Restores reasons to build MW generation without making the first powered tools inaccessible. The industrial fabricator already charges *per active lane*; adjust that rate rather than adding a second parallel accounting path. A flat multiplier over all consumers would increase grind and make bootstrap circular. |
| **High** | **Fuel/heat/engine ledger**, not a global fuel multiplier. State one playable chemical-energy scale for ordinary fuels and map flammable heat, combustible work, and engine efficiency to it; exempt fantasy fuels deliberately. Retain current grade and piston efficiency relationships by default. | Diesel/gas generated work cannot currently be read against oil-burner heat as one physical fuel budget; a shared basis closes cross-machine arbitrage and makes fuel pipelines meaningful. |
| **High** | **Fusion heat-output audit**, distinct from steam ledger: make ITER's coolant thermal content per consumed plasma packet agree with its stated useful heat and magnet/plasma-heater cost, then size exchanger and turbine trains to the result. Keep mass-conserving coolant, shield and disruption behavior. | At default one-packet/t injection, current coolant trait carries ~6.4 kW nominal against ≥1 MW input; merely increasing a tooltip's heat number cannot make fusion a useful power plant. A corrected yield must be balanced against the still-necessary fuel and cooling infrastructure. |
| **High** | **Industrial water and exhaust route validation** at nameplate: pump → treatment → boiler or light-water reactor → multistage turbine → condenser → treatment/return. Test a loaded-world steady state, blocked output, and capacity at actual ports before raising any plant rating. | The small plant is constrained by steam buffering and grades; the large plant needs high-volume pipes but should not require 100+ chemical plants for permanent makeup when condensate is returned. |
| **Medium** | **Storage tier/rate rebalance** by family. Set useful backup targets (seconds for machine buffers, minutes for ordinary batteries, longer for expensive grid storage); separate maximum charge/discharge watts from joule capacity. Preserve exotic aspirational storage, but avoid hours of 1 MW autonomy at a modest progression point. | Keeps intermittency buffers useful without replacing all generation. Revisit recipes/costs before reducing a player's existing battery capacity; save migration would be needed if implementation changes persisted caps. |
| **Medium** | **Rated transport tiers or demonstrated limits**, only after topology measurements. Express cable and pipe capacities as W and L/t where the actual network enforces them; rate in-line pumps according to transfer, not buffer-size overlay. The adjustable power diode already rates `10^level` quanta/t (level 1 = 100 W; screwdriver adjustment reaches level 11 = 1 TW), but plain cables have no similar rating. Align at least one intended line/port with each generator and boiler tier, or explicitly require parallel lines. [diode](../src/main/java/com/hbm/blocks/network/CableDiode.java#L87-L103), [rating](../src/main/java/com/hbm/blocks/network/CableDiode.java#L255-L260). | Makes distribution construction meaningful without creating invisible starvation. Generic 1-billion-L/t endpoints and unlimited plain cables currently give little reason for tiered distribution. |
| **Medium** | **Oil family throughput alignment**: retain deposit uncertainty, but reconsider 100 L/t refinery versus 10–30 L/t wells, 24,000 L product buffers and hot-oil preparation. Tune a refinery family rate and tank/port package together, or make parallel wells an explicit intended requirement. | Four pumpjacks for one refinery is plausible midgame planning; 24-second product-buffer fill under full load makes outlet handling dominant. A refinery electrical uplift should remain auxiliary to heat. |
| **Medium** | **Reactor family review**: measure default 528/ReaSim RBMK layouts, Zirnox, PWR, Watz, ITER and the heat-exchanger/boiler/turbine train separately. Keep Zirnox HU distinct; establish a HU↔J physical mapping only if fuel/steam behavior can support one. | Prevents reactor nameplates being inferred from unsupported heat-unit conversions or one idealized outlet. Preserves coolant and safety logistics. |
| **Low** | Fine-tune temperatures, condenser parasitics, ambient loss, pressure and steam density only after chain budgets and gameplay tests. Keep exotic/fantasy fluids expressly abstracted. | Improves flavor and engineering feel, but premature realism here would introduce huge water/resource burdens without solving current tier mismatches. |

**Gameplay guardrails for any later rebalance:** Preserve a starter generator path before Fabrication Machine, Chemical Reactor and fuel processing. Keep reactor fabrication and feedwater return reachable without reactor power. Let one or two starter generators support a workshop; make a midgame plant choose between multiple generators and a deliberate steam system; make a heavy complex need at least MW-class generation plus distribution and fluid headers. Favor condensate/coolant recirculation and modest makeup loss over relentless new water extraction. Build cost, process time, pollution and waste should be evaluated alongside watts. Any changed persisted capacity needs explicit save behavior; this audit proposes none.

## 5. Practical implementation sequence and validation

1. Create a machine-readable balance sheet from the existing config and recipe defaults: per-tick electrical draw/output, fluid in/out, heat in/out, input and output buffers, endpoint ratings, and tier. Add measured profiles for dynamic RTG/engine/RBMK/ITER machinery; do not assign single numbers to variable fuel or geometry.
2. Correct the steam/fuel ledgers at the **subsystem** boundary, then derive turbine/boiler nameplates and reactor output from those relationships. Calculate gross and net electricity, including powered condensation and water treatment, without changing existing efficiency coefficients opportunistically.
3. Tune electrical consumers **by technology and machine family** alongside starter and fuel generators at the new physical reference scale. Preserve first-power access, scale heavy machinery and per-lane operation, and do not change hundreds of values as unrelated one-offs.
4. Tune **transport and storage as separate passes** against the resulting producer/consumer rates. Give a source, line, port, buffer, sink, and exhaust path for each canonical plant. Do not implement a cable or pipe cap that forces dozens of parallel blocks for one intended machine.
5. Validate with source arithmetic, configurable overrides, save/load, pressure and grade matching, then in-game steady-state traces at 20 TPS and under lag. Specifically measure 528/ReaSim RBMK, Zirnox, PWR, ITER, industrial turbine, oil refinery outputs, chemical treatment, and battery discharge. This audit performed source inspection and arithmetic only; dedicated-server and in-game throughput remain unverified.

The balancing target is an *industrial hierarchy with playable logistics*: small machines remain accessible, midgame rewards networks and buffers, and late-game generation has enough meaningful loads to justify power plants. Literal real-world steam enthalpy or refinery volumes are less important than an internally coherent energy and material ledger.
